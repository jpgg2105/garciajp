Long context or RAG? What models actually use

Context windows grew from a few thousand tokens to hundreds of thousands, so why retrieve at all? Because a model that can read a long prompt doesn't use all of it equally, and it pays for every token on every question. Scroll for what the research shows.

  1. 1

    Two ways to give a model your documents

    With a long-context model you can paste everything into the prompt. With RAG you retrieve the few passages that match the question and send only those. Both answer from your documents; they differ in how much the model has to read.

  2. 2

    Position matters

    Models don't use a long context evenly. In Liu et al.'s tests with 20 documents in the prompt, accuracy was highest when the answer sat in the first or last documents and fell in the middle, for one model below answering with no documents at all.

  3. 3

    Advertised length isn't usable length

    Finding one planted sentence, the “needle in a haystack” test, stays easy for most models at long lengths. Tasks that track or combine many facts degrade much sooner: in RULER, only about half of the models that claimed 32K tokens or more held up at 32K.

  4. 4

    Cost and latency

    A long prompt is read again on every question: 150,000 tokens instead of 3,000 is 50 times the input to pay for, a slower first token and a larger KV cache. Prompt caching lowers the cost of a repeated prefix, not of the first read.

  5. 5

    Choosing

    Long context fits one long document, a small fixed set, or tasks that need the whole text. RAG fits large or changing corpora, answers with sources, access control and tight budgets. Many systems combine them: retrieve generously, then let a long-context model read more of each hit.

In short

  • Retrieval helps long-context models too: Xu et al. (2024) found a 4K-context model with retrieval comparable to a 16K-context model without it on their long-context tasks, at much less computation, and retrieval improved the larger window as well.
  • Li et al. (2024) found long context ahead of RAG on average with strong models, at a much higher cost, and proposed Self-Route: answer from retrieved chunks first, and fall back to the full context only when the model says the chunks aren't enough.
  • Test on your own documents: put the answer at different depths, and ask questions that need several facts, not only one.
  • Newer models handle long prompts better than the ones in these papers, so re-measure before relying on either result.
  • The curves in the animation show the shape of the published results, not their exact numbers.

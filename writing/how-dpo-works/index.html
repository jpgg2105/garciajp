How DPO works, and when to use it instead of RLHF

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) aligns a model on the same preference pairs as RLHF, but without a separate reward model or a reinforcement learning loop. One derivation makes this possible, and it comes with trade-offs worth knowing before you pick a method.

  1. 1

    One loss instead of two stages

    RLHF trains a reward model, then runs PPO with four models in memory. DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) optimizes the same objective with a single classification-style loss, using only the policy and a frozen reference.

  2. 2

    The policy is secretly a reward model

    The KL-regularized RLHF objective has a closed-form optimum. Solving it for the reward gives r = β·log(π/π_ref) + β·log Z(x), and Z(x) cancels when two answers to the same prompt are compared.

  3. 3

    The DPO loss

    Put that implicit reward into the Bradley–Terry model and you get one loss: raise the chosen answer's log-probability ratio relative to the rejected one's. It starts at log 2 ≈ 0.69 and falls as the margin grows.

  4. 4

    β and the offline catch

    β sets how hard the reference pulls back; smaller β lets the model move further. Because DPO trains on a fixed dataset, the model can drift into regions its preference pairs never covered.

  5. 5

    Choosing between them

    Use DPO for a simple, stable pipeline on existing pairs. Use RLHF or online RL when you need fresh samples, a reusable reward model or verifiable rewards. Variants such as IPO, KTO, ORPO, SimPO and GRPO change the loss or the data.

In short

  • No method wins everywhere. Rafailov et al. found DPO matched or beat PPO on sentiment control, summarization and dialogue; Xu et al. (2024) found well-tuned PPO ahead on harder tasks such as code generation.
  • During DPO training the chosen answers' log-probabilities often fall too; the margin grows because the rejected ones fall faster (Pal et al., 2024).
  • Online or iterative DPO samples new answers from the current model and labels them each round, recovering part of RLHF's advantage.
  • For both methods, the consistency and expertise behind the preference labels set the ceiling, not the optimizer.
  • The log-ratios in the animation are illustrative. β = 0.5 is used for visibility; TRL's default is 0.1.

How to solve problems with small experiments

When you can't know the answer ahead of time, don't plan harder. Make a guess, test it as cheaply as you can, and let each result decide the next test.

  1. 1

    Turn the idea into a guess

    Write down what you expect and how you'll measure it. 'Shorter pages get more signups' is testable; 'improve the page' isn't.

  2. 2

    Test it small and fast

    Use the smallest test that gives a real result: a day, a sample, a prototype. Cheap tests let you run many.

  3. 3

    Change one thing and compare

    Change one thing at a time, so you know what made the difference.

  4. 4

    Repeat with smaller steps

    When a result gets worse, you went too far. Step back and narrow in.

  5. 5

    Let the results find the answer

    Five cheap tests found the best setting without ever seeing the full curve.

In short

  • Decide what counts as success before you run the test, so the result can't be argued afterwards.
  • Keep a log of each guess and result. It stops you repeating tests and shows the trend.
  • It works for pricing, recipes, training plans, product features and study habits.
